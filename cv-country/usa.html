The American Standard

The US job market has strict, often unspoken rules for resumes. Including a photo or personal details (age, marital status) is a major mistake that can lead to immediate rejection for compliance reasons — many large employers use software that auto-flags applications containing this information to avoid discrimination liability. US recruiters look for concise, accomplishment-driven documents that follow a standard reverse-chronological format, and they scan fast: most résumés get well under a minute of attention on a first pass.

Key Rules for a US Resume

One page, almost always. Two pages is acceptable past ~10-15 years of experience or in academia — otherwise, one page signals you can prioritize.
No photo, no age, no marital status. Unlike much of Europe, including these isn't neutral — it's actively discouraged and can get an application auto-rejected by compliance-conscious ATS configurations.
"Resume," not "CV." In the US, "CV" specifically means the long-form academic document used for faculty/research positions — using it for a standard job application reads as a mismatch.
Every bullet starts with an action verb. "Led," "built," "reduced," "shipped" — not "Responsible for" or "Worked on."

Common Mistakes on US Resumes

An "Objective" statement. This went out of style years ago — US resumes now open with a brief summary of what you've done, not what you're hoping for.
References available upon request. Assumed by default; the line just wastes space a recruiter could spend on your actual experience.
Duties instead of results. "Managed a team" says nothing an ATS or a recruiter can rank you on. "Managed a team of 6, cutting onboarding time by 40%" does.
