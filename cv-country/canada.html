What Is the Canadian Resume Format?

Canadian resumes closely mirror the North American style — 1-2 pages, no photo, no date of birth, and a strong focus on achievements rather than duties. Unlike European CVs, Canadian employers explicitly prefer resumes that omit personal information like age, religion, or marital status to comply with human rights legislation. Key Rules for a Canadian Resume

✅ No personal photo

Unlike France or Germany, Canadian employers do not expect or want your photo. Including one may actually hurt your application.

✅ No date of birth or marital status

Canadian human rights law protects candidates from discrimination. Never include these details in a Canadian resume.

✅ 1–2 pages maximum

Keep it tight. 1 page for those with under 10 years experience; 2 pages for senior professionals. Never go over 2.

✅ Bilingual advantage (Quebec)

For Quebec-based roles, a French-language resume is often required or preferred. Our AI generates in both English and French.

✅ Quantify everything

Canadian employers respond strongly to numbers. Use CAD figures when available: "Managed $1.2M CAD budget" or "Grew team from 3 to 11."

Regional Markets and What They Look For

Canada isn't one job market, and the differences are worth reflecting in your keywords:

  • Toronto — Finance, tech, consulting, media. The most competitive market in the country; quantified results matter most here.
  • Vancouver — Tech startups, film & media, sustainability. Smaller companies, so breadth and adaptability tend to read better than narrow specialization.
  • Montreal — AI/ML, gaming, aerospace. Genuinely bilingual: many roles expect functional French even when the job is advertised in English, and Quebec's language legislation makes French a practical requirement for a lot of employers.
  • Calgary — Energy, engineering, financial services. Certifications and safety credentials are screened early.
  • Ottawa — Federal government, defense, tech. Public-sector applications often require a much longer, more detailed CV than the private-sector norm, and security clearance status is worth stating if you hold one.

If You're Applying From Outside Canada

Two things make a disproportionate difference for international applicants. First, state your work authorization — permanent resident, work permit holder, or requiring sponsorship. Employers will need to know regardless, and leaving it ambiguous tends to slow an application down rather than help it. Second, give your foreign credentials context. A degree or professional qualification a Canadian recruiter doesn't recognize is easy to undervalue; noting the equivalency, or that you've had it assessed, prevents your background from being skimmed past.

Common Mistakes on Canadian Resumes

Including a photo or personal details

Age, marital status, and photos are avoided in Canada for the same human-rights reasons as in the US. Including them can create a compliance problem for the employer, which is not a burden you want to hand them.

Ignoring French for Quebec roles

Applying to a Montreal or Quebec City posting with an English-only CV, when the role clearly operates in French, is one of the most common avoidable mistakes.

Using a US resume unchanged

The formats are close but not identical — Canadian CVs tolerate two pages more readily, and Canadian spelling conventions (favour, centre, licence) signal you're writing for this market.
