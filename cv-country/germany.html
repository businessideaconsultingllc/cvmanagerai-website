What Is a German Lebenslauf?

A Lebenslauf (German CV) is a highly structured, formal document that German recruiters expect to be precise and thorough. The most common format is the tabellarischer Lebenslauf (tabular CV), which lists information in clear, reverse-chronological order without gaps. Our AI resume builder writes your Lebenslauf content in German in this reverse-chronological structure — note that a Bewerbungsfoto isn't part of the generated document, so add one yourself before submitting if your target employer expects it.

German Lebenslauf: Key Requirements

  • 📸 Bewerbungsfoto — A professional application photo is mandatory in Germany
  • 📅 Exact dates — Month and year for every position (no gaps tolerated)
  • ✉️ Anschreiben — A formal cover letter is always expected alongside the Lebenslauf
  • 📄 Strict reverse chronology — Most recent experience listed first, always
  • 🔢 No gaps — Unexplained employment gaps are viewed negatively. Always account for every period

German Lebenslauf Format: Section by Section

1. Persönliche Daten (Personal Information)

Name, address, phone, email, date and place of birth, nationality. Include your LinkedIn and GitHub if applicable.

2. Berufserfahrung (Work Experience)

Reverse chronological. Include company name, location, job title, dates (MM.YYYY), and 3–5 achievement bullet points per role.

3. Ausbildung (Education)

University degree, Ausbildung (vocational training), Abitur. Include GPA if above 2.0 (German scale). Mention scholarships or honors.

4. Kenntnisse (Skills)

Language skills with level (B2, C1, fluent), IT skills, and any specialized technical certifications.

5. Ort, Datum, Unterschrift

German CVs traditionally end with the place, date, and your handwritten signature (or digital equivalent).

Common Mistakes on a German Lebenslauf

Leaving an unexplained gap

Even a short gap between roles should be labeled — travel, further study, caregiving. An unexplained blank period reads as a red flag to German HR far more than it would elsewhere.

Skipping the photo where one is expected

The Bewerbungsfoto convention is fading in some industries (especially tech and startups) but is still the norm in traditional sectors — check what's typical for the specific employer before deciding to omit it.

Sending a Lebenslauf without an Anschreiben

In Germany the cover letter isn't optional the way it can feel elsewhere — submitting a CV alone often reads as an incomplete application.
