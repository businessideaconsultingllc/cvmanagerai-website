4 Tips for a Designer Resume That Passes ATS

1. Link to your portfolio prominently

ATS might not see your images, but it will see your URL. Make sure your Portfolio link is in the header in plain text.

2. Use standard font names

Creative fonts can break ATS parsers. Stick to web-standard fonts for the document itself; let your portfolio show your typography skills.

3. Describe the "Why" behind your designs

Instead of "Made logos," write "Developed 3 logo concepts that aligned with target demographic, increasing user trust by 20%."

4. Avoid images of text

Never save your resume as a flat JPG or PNG. The ATS cannot read the text inside an image. Use our AI to export a search-friendly PDF.

The Real Tension in a Designer's Resume

The resume and the portfolio do different jobs

Your portfolio proves taste. Your resume proves you can work inside constraints — deadlines, brand guidelines, stakeholder feedback. Trying to make the resume itself the visual showcase usually costs you both.

"Creative" without a client or a constraint

A redesign is more convincing with the brief attached: what the client needed, what limited you (budget, existing brand, timeline), and what you shipped anyway. That's the story hiring managers are actually screening for.

Generalist framing for a specialist search

Brand identity, UI/UX, and motion are different hiring searches even when one person does all three. Leading with whichever discipline matches the specific job posting gets you read as the right fit faster.
