Why Teaching Experience Is Hard to Put on a Resume

Teaching work doesn't naturally translate into resume bullet points — the job is relational and ongoing, not project-based. That makes it easy to default to duties ("responsible for lesson planning and classroom management") that describe every teacher equally. District ATS systems are usually configured to match specific pedagogical and administrative terms, so vague duty descriptions can filter out strong candidates before a human ever reads the application.

• Led department-wide adoption of a shared Google Classroom template, cutting new-teacher onboarding time in half

Tips for an ATS-Ready Teacher Resume

1. List your certification and state exactly

"State-certified, Grades 6-12 Math" is a specific, matchable line — "certified teacher" alone often isn't specific enough for ATS filters.

2. Turn outcomes into numbers where you can

Test score changes, attendance improvements, or program participation growth give a reviewer something concrete instead of "dedicated to student success."

3. Name your subject and grade band clearly

"Elementary" vs. "secondary," and the specific subject, changes which openings you match — make sure both appear near the top.

4. Include compliance work as its own line

IEP, 504, or ELL support experience is often screened for separately from general classroom duties — don't let it disappear inside a paragraph.

Where Teacher Resumes Usually Fall Short

Passion statements instead of evidence

"Passionate about student success" is true of nearly every applicant. A specific change you made and what happened afterward does the same job more convincingly.

No distinction between grade bands

Elementary and secondary teaching draw on genuinely different skills. A resume that reads as generic across both makes it harder for a reviewer to place you correctly.

Extracurricular work left off entirely

Coaching, club advising, and committee work often signal the collaboration and initiative districts screen for beyond the classroom — worth a line if it's real.
