What Makes an Accounting Resume Actually Work

Accounting resumes carry a specific tension: the work is precise and compliance-driven, but a resume that only lists duties ("responsible for month-end close") reads as interchangeable with every other candidate. Firms and finance teams are scanning for two things at once — the regulatory vocabulary their ATS is configured to match (GAAP, IFRS, SOX), and evidence that your work actually changed a number: an error rate, a close timeline, a cost line.

Tips for an ATS-Ready Accounting Resume

1. Name your certifications exactly

"CPA," "ACCA," or "CMA" as standalone terms — not buried in a sentence — so ATS systems configured to filter on licensure catch them.

2. Attach a number to every process you touched

Close timelines, error rates, dollar amounts reconciled. "Managed reconciliations" says nothing an ATS or a hiring manager can act on.

3. Name the software, not just "accounting software"

SAP, Oracle, NetSuite, and QuickBooks are all screened for separately — list the ones you've actually used.

4. Separate compliance work from operational work

Audit and SOX compliance experience is often screened for on its own line — don't let it get lost inside general bullet points.

Where Accounting Resumes Usually Fall Short

"Responsible for" instead of what changed

Accounting work is inherently about accuracy and control — but a resume that only describes the process, not what improved because of it, gives a reviewer nothing to differentiate you on.

Certifications listed without context

A CPA license belongs where it's easy to find — usually right under your name or in a dedicated line — not buried at the bottom of an education section.

No distinction between industries

Public accounting, corporate/industry accounting, and tax specialization are different searches even when the core skill overlaps. Leading with the one that matches the posting gets you read correctly, faster.
