Why "Good Communicator" Doesn't Work on a Resume

Every job candidate claims to be a "good communicator." The phrase has become so generic that ATS systems barely register it, and recruiters actively roll their eyes at it. What hiring managers actually want to see is evidence of communication skills — specific situations, audiences, and measurable outcomes. Types of Communication Skills to Highlight by Role

For Leadership Roles

Focus on: executive presentations, strategic communication, change management communication, and crisis communication. Mention audience size and seniority level.

For Technical Roles (Developers, Engineers)

Highlight: technical documentation, API documentation, cross-functional communication between engineering and non-technical stakeholders, code reviews.

For Customer-Facing Roles (Sales, Support)

Emphasize: client relationship management, negotiation outcomes, conflict resolution, NPS scores, CX ratings, response time SLAs met.

For Marketing & Content Roles

Show: editorial output (articles written, campaigns launched), audience reach, speaking engagements, media coverage secured, or email open rates achieved.
